The strongest thread today is the gap between "it works" and "it's reliable" — and it runs through four of five picks. Martin Fowler and Thoughtworks documenting Bayer's PRINCE platform is the standout: a named pharma company, a named system, live since early 2024, with concrete engineering principles for agentic RAG in a regulated environment. If you read one thing this week, it's that.

AWS is framing the same problem from the hyperscaler side — agents fail in production because they lack business context and security posture, and their two new services are a direct answer. Paired with lcamtuf's essay on LLM quasi-determinism (the "100k Whys" book cover test) and the engineer's taxonomy for rejecting AI-generated code even when CI passes, you have a coherent picture: AI is fast to deploy and unreliable to trust without deliberate engineering discipline.

The Berkeley grade-inflation study rounds it out from a different angle — large-scale evidence that AI shifts effort rather than adding capability. Worth keeping in mind for AI fluency programmes where the risk is substitution, not augmentation.
